TL;DR: I talked about BGP at SEE 14 in Belgrade. 🇷🇸 

Someone should have warned me how addictive Network Operator Groups are.

Last week, I attended the South East Europe #SEE14 meeting in Belgrade, Serbia, where I presented some work on BGP Maximum-Prefix Limits.

After crunching 3.4 TB of full-year 2025 RIS data and blending it with even more data 🫠, the takeaway is that although this limit is intended to prevent route leak propagation, it is often left outdated, dropping BGP sessions even when the prefixes increase is organic or traffic engineering decisions are applied.

In some cases, it can disconnect the network from major providers, increasing the risk of poor routing decisions, higher latency, or SLA violations. 

It would be great to revisit how this is configured and allow dynamic negotiation between peers. Hopefully, this measurement can kick off that conversation again.

**Program highlights:** ASPA, BGP Routes platform, IXP support, Data Center operational issues, Satellites, and AI agents for root cause analysis. Easy peasy to start talking during the coffee breaks. 

**On the social side:** familiar faces and new ones. I tried to learn the traditional dance, Kolo (total fail 🫠 from my part, maybe next time). It is always crazy how a conversation that starts with "nice presentation, I wonder if you looked at ..." extends to completely unpredictable topics.
